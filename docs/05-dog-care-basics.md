# Dog Care Basics

This is general information to help dog lovers. It is not veterinary advice. If you are worried about the health of a real dog, call a veterinarian.

## Food and water

Dogs need fresh water at all times and a complete, balanced dog food suited to their age: puppy, adult or senior. Portion sizes depend on the dog's size, age and activity, and the feeding guide on the food packet is a good starting point. Puppies eat three or four small meals a day, and most adult dogs do well on two meals a day. Keep treats to a small share of the daily food, because dogs gain weight easily. A healthy dog has ribs you can feel but not see, and a visible waist when viewed from above.

## Foods that are dangerous for dogs

Never feed a dog these foods:

- Chocolate, especially dark chocolate and baking chocolate
- Grapes and raisins
- Onions, garlic and chives
- Xylitol, a sweetener found in some sugar-free gum, sweets and peanut butters
- Macadamia nuts
- Alcohol and caffeine
- Cooked bones, which can splinter

If a dog eats something dangerous, call a vet or an emergency animal poison service straight away.

## Exercise

All dogs need daily exercise and mental stimulation, but the amount differs by breed. Herding, sporting and working breeds such as the Border Collie, Labrador Retriever and Siberian Husky often need an hour or more of activity each day. Many toy breeds are happy with shorter walks and play. Flat-faced breeds such as the Pug, Bulldog and Boxer overheat easily and should not exercise hard in hot weather. Games such as fetch, tug, hide-and-seek and puzzle feeders keep a dog's mind busy.

## Grooming

- **Short smooth coats** (Beagle, Boxer, Whippet) need a quick brush weekly.
- **Double coats** (Husky, Golden Retriever, German Shepherd) shed heavily and need brushing several times a week.
- **Long or curly coats** (Poodle, Shih Tzu, Maltese) can mat and need frequent brushing and regular professional trims.
- **Wiry coats** (many terriers) are often hand-stripped or clipped.

Check and clean the ears if they are dirty, trim nails every few weeks so they do not touch the floor, and bathe a dog only when needed, with a shampoo made for dogs.

## Teeth

Brush a dog's teeth with a dog toothpaste several times a week, or daily if you can. Never use human toothpaste. Dental chews and regular vet dental checks help prevent gum disease, which is very common in dogs.

## Vet care

Puppies need a series of vaccinations, and adult dogs need regular boosters and a yearly check-up. Dogs also need regular treatment against fleas, ticks and worms, and in many areas heartworm prevention. Neutering or spaying is a decision to discuss with a vet. A microchip with up-to-date contact details is the best way to be reunited with a lost dog, and a collar with an identity tag is also important.

## Weather safety

Never leave a dog in a parked car. The inside of a car can become dangerously hot within minutes, even on a mild day. In hot weather give shade, water and walks at cooler times of day. In very cold weather, short-coated, small and elderly dogs may need a coat, and paws should be wiped after walking on salted roads.

## Life stages and lifespan

Small breeds often live longer, commonly 12 to 16 years, while giant breeds such as the Great Dane or Irish Wolfhound often live around 7 to 10 years. Senior dogs benefit from gentler exercise, comfortable bedding and vet checks twice a year.

## Signs that a dog should see a vet

Contact a vet if a dog refuses food for more than a day, vomits repeatedly, has diarrhoea that does not clear up, is limping, seems very tired, has trouble breathing, drinks far more than usual or has swollen or painful areas.
