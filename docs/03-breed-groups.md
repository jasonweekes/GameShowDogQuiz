# Dog Breed Groups

Kennel clubs sort breeds into groups by the job the breed was developed to do. The groups below follow the common American Kennel Club style of grouping. Other clubs sort a few breeds differently, so treat the groups as a helpful guide rather than a strict rule. Many of these breeds appear in the Breed Rush quiz.

## Sporting group

Sporting dogs were bred to help hunters find and bring back birds. They are usually friendly, energetic and keen to please, and they need plenty of exercise. Quiz examples: Labrador Retriever, Golden Retriever, Chesapeake Bay Retriever, Curly-Coated Retriever, Flat-Coated Retriever, English Setter, Gordon Setter, Irish Setter, Cocker Spaniel, English Springer Spaniel, Brittany, Weimaraner, Vizsla and the German Shorthaired Pointer.

## Hound group

Hounds were bred to chase game. Sight hounds spot prey and run it down with speed: the Greyhound, Whippet, Saluki, Afghan Hound, Borzoi, Irish Wolfhound, Scottish Deerhound and Ibizan Hound are sight hounds. Scent hounds follow a trail with their noses: the Beagle, Basset Hound, Bloodhound, Otterhound, Plott Hound, Redbone Coonhound and Walker Hound are scent hounds. The Dachshund was bred to follow badgers underground, and the Basenji is a barkless African hound known for its yodel-like sound. The Rhodesian Ridgeback is named for the strip of hair growing backwards along its spine.

## Working group

Working dogs were bred to guard, pull sleds or carts, and rescue people. They are big, strong and intelligent. Quiz examples: Boxer, Doberman Pinscher, Rottweiler, Great Dane, Siberian Husky, Alaskan Malamute, Samoyed, Akita, Bernese Mountain Dog, Saint Bernard, Newfoundland, Mastiffs (English, Tibetan and the Bullmastiff), Great Pyrenees, Komondor, Kuvasz, Giant Schnauzer and Leonberger. Because they are strong, working breeds benefit from early training and socialization.

## Terrier group

Terriers were bred to hunt and dig out vermin such as rats and foxes. They are feisty, brave and energetic, and many have a wiry coat. Quiz examples: Airedale, Border Terrier, Cairn Terrier, Scottish Terrier, West Highland White Terrier, Yorkshire Terrier, Norfolk Terrier, Norwich Terrier, Bedlington Terrier, Kerry Blue Terrier, Lakeland Terrier, Soft Coated Wheaten Terrier, Welsh Terrier, Fox Terrier, Russell Terrier, Silky Terrier, Australian Terrier and the Staffordshire Bull Terrier. The Airedale is the largest terrier.

## Toy group

Toy breeds are small companions that are easy to carry. They can be big characters in small bodies. Quiz examples: Chihuahua, Pomeranian, Pug, Maltese, Shih Tzu, Papillon, Pekingese, Havanese, Affenpinscher, Miniature Pinscher and the Italian Greyhound. The Chihuahua is often called the smallest dog breed in the world.

## Non-Sporting group

This group is a mix of breeds that do not fit elsewhere. Quiz examples: Bulldog (English), French Bulldog, Poodle, Dalmatian, Chow Chow, Shar-Pei, Lhasa Apso, Shiba Inu, Keeshond, Schipperke, Bichon Frise, Boston Terrier and the Tibetan Terrier. The Dalmatian is born white and develops its spots as a puppy.

## Herding group

Herding dogs were bred to gather and move livestock. They are smart, agile and need both exercise and a job to do. Quiz examples: German Shepherd, Belgian Malinois, Belgian Tervuren, Groenendael, Border Collie, Rough Collie, Australian Shepherd, Shetland Sheepdog, Pembroke Welsh Corgi, Cardigan Welsh Corgi, Old English Sheepdog, Briard, Australian Cattle Dog, Australian Kelpie, Bouvier des Flandres and the Entlebucher Mountain Dog. The Border Collie is widely considered one of the most trainable breeds.

## Wild, landrace and mixed dogs

Breed Rush also includes some dogs that are not formal kennel-club breeds. The Dingo is a wild dog of Australia. The African wild dog and the Dhole are wild canids that are not domestic dogs. Several Indian dogs in the quiz list, such as the Indian Pariah dog, Rajapalayam, Chippiparai, Kombai, Mudhol Hound and Bakharwal dog, are regional or landrace types. Poodle crosses such as the Labradoodle, Cockapoo, Cavapoo and Puggle are popular crossbreeds rather than recognized breeds.

## Sizes at a glance

Very small dogs, under about 5 kilograms (11 pounds), include the Chihuahua and Yorkshire Terrier. Medium dogs, roughly 10 to 25 kilograms (22 to 55 pounds), include the Beagle, Border Collie and Cocker Spaniel. Giant breeds, which can weigh 50 kilograms (110 pounds) or more, include the Great Dane, Saint Bernard, Newfoundland, Mastiff and Irish Wolfhound. The Irish Wolfhound and Great Dane are among the tallest breeds.
